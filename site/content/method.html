The viewer plots the low modes of one operator per network. This page says what that operator is, what the viewer's options do, and which steps are fits added for display. The per-site construction it starts from is described in full on the Coherent Modes site, which this viewer was split from.

Networks, probe images, sites

  • Networks. ResNet-20, ResNet-32 and ResNet-56 (two seeds each) and one VGG-16-BN trained on CIFAR-10 are shown across saved checkpoints. The 14 additional ResNet-20 networks from X1 each have one final checkpoint: their own 12-site k-NN 15 operator in relative site units at $t^*$. Images, labels, class counts, normalisation and augmentation can differ across those training pipelines.
  • Probe images. The same $N = 2{,}000$ CIFAR-10 test images, 200 per class, for every network. Class labels are used only to colour the plot, never to build an operator.
  • Sites. The places in a network where activations are read: for the ResNets the stem, each residual block, the pooled features and the logits. A network with $L$ sites gives $L$ sets of activations of the probe.
  • Walk at one site. At site $l$, activations are flattened, centred over the probe and normalised, and a graph joins each image to its 15 nearest neighbours by cosine distance. Scaling that graph to be symmetric and doubly stochastic and averaging it with the identity gives a walk $M_l$ on the probe images (details). Nothing is trained on top of the network.

One operator through depth

The viewer uses one operator for all of a network's sites at once. (The through-depth experiment on the Coherent Modes site measures the same operator on ResNet-20.) Its functions $f = (f_0, \dots, f_{L-1})$ have one slice per site, each a function on the probe images, and its energy charges for roughness within each site and for change between neighbouring sites:

$$ E_t(f) \;=\; \sum_{l} \frac{f_l^\top (I - M_l)\, f_l}{c_l} \;+\; t \sum_{l} \lVert f_{l+1} - f_l \rVert^2 . $$

This is the supra-Laplacian of Froyland, Kalia and Koltai, with the identity as the link between sites: image $i$ at one site is image $i$ at the next. Its low modes give each image a row of $K$ numbers at every site, all in one $K$-dimensional space. That row is what the viewer plots. It offers three variants.

  • Relative site units (the default): $c_l$ is the mean of site $l$'s first eight nontrivial values of $1 - \lambda$, the least mean roughness any eight orthonormal mean-zero functions can have at that site.
  • Native walk units: $c_l = 1$.
  • Paper transition: the matrix of Trower, Djurdjevac Conrad and Klus, used as is.

Why relative units are the default rather than the paper's matrix

Against the paper's matrix. For these symmetric walks its eigenproblem has an exact energy (SPEC_SPACETIME.md, S2): $1 - \lambda = \sum_l \sum_{i,j} (M_l)_{ij}\,(f_l(i) - f_{l+1}(j))^2$. Image $i$ at one site is linked to a random neighbour $j$ at the next. Roughness within a site and change between sites are therefore paid through the same walk step, and nothing sets their relative price. Three consequences follow.

  • Its graph is not the identity link, so "the same image, followed through depth" is not what it encodes.
  • Site $l$'s walk is used for both slices $l$ and $l+1$, and the last site's walk never enters.
  • Its spectrum mixes in slice-constant ("temporal") modes that say nothing about the images. The paper filters them by inspection, so the number of groups cannot be read from a gap.

The additive operator above keeps the two charges separate, with an explicit rate $t$. The slice-constant modes form an exactly invariant sector and are removed (S1). Its two limits are objects the Coherent Modes site already uses: as $t \to 0$, the separate sites; as $t \to \infty$, a block average over all sites (S3). As $t$ grows, the trade between roughness and change is exact and monotone (S6). The paper's matrix is still shown alongside, with the same readings, as a comparison.

Against native units. With $c_l = 1$ the price of roughness at a site is set by that site's walk. In ResNet-20 seed 0 the late walks are much smoother: smooth functions are about 8 times cheaper at logits than in the middle blocks. So the cheapest modes pile onto the last sites. At the final checkpoint, b9, pool and logits hold 0.99 of the 16-mode space at $t^*/64$ and 0.55 at $t^*$. That pile-up is mostly the gap landscape, not image geometry: a scalar control that sees only each site's gap approximately reproduces mode $1$'s depth profile (S5; see the experiment for where it does not). Relative units divide out that one number per site, so each site's cheapest eight-frame costs the same, and leave everything else. The space then spreads over all sites: at $t^*$, each site holds between 0.050 and 0.121, against an even share of 0.083. For comparison, the paper's matrix puts 0.49 on b9 to logits.

What relative units do not do. They do not equalise spectral gaps or best cuts, and the eight-mode reference is a convention. The default is a choice about what counts as "equally rough at every site", not a result. The coupling $t$ is also a free rate. The six stops are multiples of the balance coupling $t^*$, which is a ruler, not an optimum.

SEBA sets through depth

The viewer can also colour by SEBA run once on the stacked low space. The input is the constant eigenfunction plus the first $r-1$ modes, each a vector of length $L \cdot N$ over all (site, image) pairs, re-orthonormalised after the int8 rounding of the shipped data. The algorithm, threshold ($0.99/\sqrt{LN}$), sign, scaling and reliability order are those of the Coherent Modes explorer's SEBA. For the paper's matrix the shipped slices are of $B^{1/2} f$, so its constant enters as $B^{1/2}\mathbf 1$. Each of the $r$ sparse vectors is a set of (site, image) pairs whose support can grow, persist or shrink along depth. The hard label of image $i$ at site $l$ is the set whose vector is largest there, if positive, else unassigned; so a set's count at a site can also fall because another vector wins there while its own support persists. The modes enter unrescaled, since only then are they orthonormal. The construction's other slice-constant modes, which carry no information about the images, are left out. Froyland, Kalia and Koltai also apply SEBA to supra-Laplacian eigenvectors. Their procedure differs: it pairs each nontrivial spatial eigenvector with a slice-norm companion and screens out spurious outputs, and neither step is reproduced here. Each network runs its own SEBA. In the side-by-side view, other networks' sets are matched one-to-one to the reference's by overlap, greedily, for colour only. SEBA is a readout of the modes, like the $k$-means sets, not part of the construction.

Comparing networks, and the baselines

Each network's operator is built on its own, from that network's walks alone; nothing ties two networks together in the construction. To show them side by side, the viewer makes two post-hoc fits, both labelled and switchable.

  • Depth matching: either fractional depth (site $l$ of $L$ sits at $l/(L-1)$), or DTW on the subspace distance $1 - \lVert Q_A^\top Q_B \rVert_F^2 / K$ between the $K$-column spaces of two sites, which ignores rotations.
  • Coordinates: one orthogonal $K \times K$ rotation per network onto the reference network, fitted by Procrustes over the matched sites and the same at every depth, so each network's own motion is unchanged.

The relative residual after the rotation, $\sum \lVert Y_B - Y_{\mathrm{ref}} \rVert^2 / \sum \lVert Y_{\mathrm{ref}} \rVert^2$, is shown per network and per reference site; 0 means identical coordinates. It measures how far one network's embedding transfers to another's, under these two fits. The viewer uses the full chain; its fitted rotation and residual are not the X1 joint agreement statistic.

As baselines, the viewer also shows ordinary embeddings computed at each site separately: the leading principal coordinates of the centred cosine activations (PCA), and a 2-d UMAP. Nothing links one site's baseline coordinates to the next, so they are chained along depth by Procrustes, each site rotated onto the previous rotated one: the usual way to animate independent embeddings. Any smoothness of their motion through depth comes from that chaining fit. In the through-depth modes, one mode is a single function over all sites. The X1 additions do not ship PCA or UMAP baselines or k-means label planes. SEBA is computed in the viewer from the shipped modes, so it is still available.

Coordinates for a new image

The probe, kernel and modes stay fixed. At site $l$, flatten the new activation of image $x$, subtract the probe mean and divide by its centred norm (floored at $10^{-12}$), just as for each probe image $j$. Write $d_{xj}$ for one minus the clipped dot product of those rows (cosine distance when both are unit length). Below, site subscripts are omitted in the kernel rules. See xp/xlib.py:probe_gram, cross_cos and invert/modal_invert.py:gram.

One kernel row per site

Let $P$ be the fitted non-lazy walk on the probe and $v_j$ the symmetric Sinkhorn scale for probe image $j$. Compute weights $w_j$ by one of the rules below; then $p(x,j)=v_j w_j / \sum_i v_i w_i$. There is no new Sinkhorn fit. To replay a probe image, mask its self-edge; the resulting row reproduces its row of $P$ up to numerical error (and the choice of neighbours at a k-NN boundary tie).

  • k-NN. Set $w_j=1$ if $j$ is among $x$'s $k$ nearest probe images or $d_{xj}\le r_j$, where $r_j$ is $j$'s fitted $k$th-neighbour distance. Otherwise use the floor $10^{-6}$; a masked entry gets zero. See invert/dm.py:knn_kernel, knn_oos_rows and xp/xlib.py:knn_rows_t.
  • Shannon perplexity and participation ratio. Fit $\beta_x$ in the conditional $q_x(j)=\exp[-\beta_x(d_{xj}-d_{x,\min})]/Z_x$, with $d_{x,\min}$ the closest unmasked distance and $Z_x$ the sum of those unnormalised weights over unmasked probe images. The Shannon rule sets $\exp H(q_x)=\xi$ with $H(q)=-\sum_j q(j)\log q(j)$; the participation-ratio rule sets $1/\sum_j q_x(j)^2=\xi$. Here $\xi$ is the chosen effective neighbour count, not the count of the final walk row. Use the probe's frozen reverse weight and average the two directions:
    $$ \begin{aligned} \widetilde q_j(x)&=\exp[-\beta_j(d_{xj}-d_{j,\min})]/Z_j,\\ w_j&=\tfrac12\bigl(q_x(j)+\widetilde q_j(x)\bigr). \end{aligned} $$
    The reverse weights are not renormalised over $j$ before averaging. The factor $1/2$ cancels when normalising $p$; the code sums the two directions and normalises in the log domain. See invert/dm.py:_cond_rows, ea_kernel, ea_oos_rows, xp/dm.py:ea_kernel and xp/xlib.py:_cond_rows_t, ea_rows_t.

From rows to coordinates

Site-local (Nyström). For a nonconstant eigenpair $P_l\phi_{l,r}=\lambda_{l,r}\phi_{l,r}$ with $\lambda_{l,r}\ne 0$, use $z_{l,r}(x)=\sum_j p_l(x,j)\phi_{l,r}(j)/\lambda_{l,r}$. On the probe this gives $\phi_{l,r}(x)$. These axes are fitted at each site separately. The xp fit gets the same eigenvectors from $M_l=(I+P_l)/2$ and converts each lazy eigenvalue to $\lambda_{l,r}=2\lambda^{\rm lazy}_{l,r}-1$. See xp/xlib.py:site_spectra, fit_op, Ext.site and invert/dm.py:site_forward.

Sources and joint coordinates. Let $(\mu_k,f^{(k)})$ be a low eigenpair of the through-depth operator above, on slice-mean-zero functions. In relative site units $c_l$ is the mean of the first eight nonconstant lazy gaps $1-\lambda^{\rm lazy}_{l,r}$ (smallest first). The depth coupling $t$ is set to $t^*$, where the lowest slice-mean-zero eigenvalue meets the first nonconstant depth-only eigenvalue $4t\sin^2(\pi/(2L))$. With $A_{\rm path}$ the adjacency matrix between consecutive sites and $\deg=(1,2,\dots,2,1)$ for a chain with at least two sites, the solve is

$$ \begin{aligned} s_{l,k}(x)&=\sum_j p_l(x,j) f_l^{(k)}(j),\\ T_k&=\operatorname{diag}_l\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2c_l}+t\deg_l-\mu_k\right)-tA_{\rm path},\\ T_k y_{\cdot,k}(x)&=\bigl(s_{l,k}(x)/(2c_l)\bigr)_{l=0}^{L-1}. \end{aligned} $$

The source only uses the activation at site $l$. Although $T_k$ is tridiagonal, solving with it mixes sources across the chain. The exact identity, on and off the probe, is $s_{l,k}(x)=2c_l[T_k y_{\cdot,k}(x)]_l$. For probe image $i$, the eigen-equation with $(I-M_l)/c_l=(I-P_l)/(2c_l)$ gives $y_{l,k}(i)=f_l^{(k)}(i)$ and $s_{l,k}(i)=(P_l f_l^{(k)})(i)$.

Code. spacetime.py:apply_L, modes, balance_direct defines the spectrum and coupling; invert/dm.py:joint_modes, joint_forward uses NumPy, with a condition number per mode, and invert/modal_invert.py:joint saves the unscaled source as onestep. xp/xlib.py:Ext uses batched Torch rows and solves, with $b=s/(2c)$ as its intermediate instead. The original X3/X4 runs used Shannon or k-NN; the current xp code also implements participation ratio. NumPy's argpartition and Torch's topk can resolve k-NN ties differently.
