Where do other datasets, noisy and adversarial images land?

Tested: one CIFAR-10 ResNet-20 checkpoint. Overlay and support plots use the frozen 2,000-image CIFAR-10 probe P2 pr300. The quantitative decoding comparison uses the 8,000-image probe P8 pr400. Neither probe nor decoder was fitted to the shifted inputs.

Shifted inputs need not be harder to decode. At b4, SVHN has higher mean centred cosine than held-out CIFAR-10 ({{fact:x.inv-ood.svhn.b4}} vs {{fact:x.inv-ood.h.b4}}, P8 pr400, K = 64). Each decode is compared with the activation the network actually produced on that input, not with its clean counterpart or a class target.

Caveats

  • Higher cosine on SVHN does not establish better support or greater information retention. Mean shifts, activation spread and decoder bias could all change this comparison; their contributions were not separated.
  • Audit correction: For the full report's nearest-centroid readout, the joint centroids were fitted on the probe, but the source centroids were fitted on the second half of H. Those readouts are not the same labelled reference fit.
  • The Gaussian-noise set is not a perturbation-size-matched PGD control. PGD results describe one attack setup and include unsuccessful attempts; they do not establish robustness. Its settings also differ from the separate X3 experiment.
  • Nearest-probe cosine and a calibrated decode-error-versus-coverage curve are not shown. The saved normalised row weights do not identify absolute nearest-probe cosine.
  • One network, one checkpoint and one probe draw; no uncertainty over runs.
