Exploratory results from one overnight session (21–22 November 2026) and one later run (the autoencoders, 26 November): one architecture (ResNet-20), one or two seeds. Each entry asks one question, answers it in a sentence or two, and leads with the diffusion embedding, with the experiment's own images drawn into it.

Every number is read from the stored outputs when the site is built. A second model audited the findings and definitions against the code; corrections that changed a claim are marked audit.

A joint coordinate at one site is computed from every site's kernel row, so questions about a single site use source or site-local coordinates. Descriptive comparisons survived checks better than attempts to recover or steer mid-network activations through coordinates.

  • Attacks part from noise at b7. Untargeted 8/255 attacks move about as far as same-budget sign noise at b6, then further from b7 (Shannon: {{fact:x.x3-adversarial.disp.u8.b7}} vs {{fact:x.x3-adversarial.disp.n8.b7}}), under all three kernels; a single image's switch site depends on the kernel. Adversarial
  • Natural-image training embeds alike. CIFAR-100 and ImageNet‑32 networks embed the CIFAR-10 probe similarly to CIFAR-10 ones (b9 agreement {{fact:x.x1-data.pair.c10_in32.b9}} for ImageNet-32); SVHN networks agree less ({{fact:x.x1-data.pair.c10_svhn.b9}}) and already differ at the stem. Training data
  • Reconstruction parts from classification at the stem. The classifiers' ResNet-20 trained as an autoencoder shares {{fact:x.ae-objective.nn.ae10_clf_diff.stem}} of a classifier's 15 nearest neighbours at the stem and {{fact:x.ae-objective.nn.ae10_clf_diff.b9}} at b9; two classifiers share {{fact:x.ae-objective.nn.clf_seeds.stem}} and {{fact:x.ae-objective.nn.clf_seeds.b9}}. Autoencoders
  • Decoding works at pool, not b4. Given the clean trajectory, kernel ridge recovers activation direction with cosine {{fact:x.inv-decode.large.pool}} at pool and {{fact:x.inv-decode.large.b4}} at the much larger b4, ahead of latent harmonics on mean cosine. Decoding
  • Wavelets do not improve the inverse. On the same coordinates, diffusion wavelets in place of harmonics leave the decode essentially unchanged, and the activations are not sparser in them; a spectral cut-off with a bounded reciprocal exists, but reconstruction, function and round trip never agree on one. Wavelets
  • Matching sources barely steers. At b4, a near-exact oracle match to a donor's source coordinates did not improve the patched joint match and moved the donor's class probability only from {{fact:x.inv-edits.b4.residual}} to {{fact:x.inv-edits.b4.match}}. Edits
  • No walls in the activations. On {{fact:x.x4-paths.paths.total}} straight lines between two images' activations, no recorded later site moved more than {{fact:x.x4-paths.window.max}} of its distance in any sixteenth of the line, though class probabilities can still change sharply. Paths
  • Kernel: participation ratio. Chosen because Shannon modes on the larger probe put up to {{fact:x.inv-kernel.top2.p8.ea400}} of their squared weight on two images; it did not win every stability test. Kernel
  • The sparse k-NN walk is exact. As a sparse matrix plus a rank-one term, stored walks shrink from {{fact:x.solver.storage.dense}} to {{fact:x.solver.storage.sparse}} MB; larger-probe costs are projections. Solver

How far to trust it. No entry reports a bootstrap or confidence interval, and several scores reuse images that built the operator. A new image's row uses a frozen reverse affinity, so it is not built exactly like a probe row. The adversarial noise control matches the L∞ budget, not L2 size.

Open questions. Do these results hold on a fresh probe, another architecture and L2-matched noise? Do cross-fitted new-image rows change the decoding results?
