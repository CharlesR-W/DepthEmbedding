How a line is made

A wall here means a short stretch of the line where the activations downstream do most of their moving. The network is continuous, so nothing jumps literally; the question is how concentrated the change gets. With pixels as the start site, the line is a cross-fade between the two images.

No concentrated movement in the activations

At every recorded later site, the activations move along the line at a fairly even pace. To see this, add up how far the activation at a later site moves between neighbouring points, starting from s = 0, and divide by the total. At an even pace this share of distance covered follows the diagonal; at a wall it would stay flat, shoot up, then stay flat. The fastest sixteenth is the largest share covered within any stretch one sixteenth of the line long: {{fact:x.x4-paths.grid.uniform}} for an even pace, {{fact:x.x4-paths.grid.jump}} for a single jump.

Class probabilities can still change sharply

Movement is not concentrated, but the output probabilities can change within a sixteenth of the line, because the softmax saturates. The figure adapts the plots of Stefan Heimersheim and Jake Mendel (activation plateaus and sensitive directions, whose x-axis is perturbation size rather than position along a line). The y-axis is the KL divergence between the output probabilities at the start of the line and at s, in nats. When the start is confidently classified, it is close to minus the log of the probability left on image a's class.

The same pair drawn at b9, where the logits move evenly on a straight line, also rises steeply. While one class leads by a clear margin its probability stays near 1; once another class overtakes it, that probability collapses. Across all lines drawn at b9, the largest drop in starting-class probability within one sixteenth was {{fact:x.x4-paths.steep.b9.drop_pp}} percentage points. So a flat stretch followed by a steep rise does not need a nonlinear network.

Three lines up close

Each example follows one pair drawn at four start sites: what the network predicts, how fast the logits move, and how strongly the network stretches small changes, all along the line. The maps below show where the line drawn at b3 runs relative to the classes it passes through.

Lines drawn early detour through other classes

Detours become rarer when the line is drawn after b3. A detour is a stretch where the network's top class is neither endpoint's class. Same-class lines that leave their class spend a median {{fact:x.x4-paths.leave_stretch.pixels.median}} to {{fact:x.x4-paths.leave_stretch.b3.median}} of the line outside it (drawn at pixels, stem, b3 or b6): the excursions are long, though this measures how long they last, not how sharply they begin. None of {{fact:x.x4-paths.pairs.per_kind}} same-class lines drawn at b8 left its class. Drawn at b9, none can: the rest of the network is affine, so the logits move on a straight line, and the logits where one class wins form a convex set. A different-class line drawn at b9 can still clip a third class, and {{fact:x.x4-paths.third.b9.share}} do.

In the embedding, crossings bunch up

Lines drawn straight at b9, whose activations move exactly evenly, reach {{fact:x.x4-paths.embstep.emb_b9}} in the map. The bunching is consistent with the probe's class clusters at b9 and with the kernel used to place new points; this comparison does not separate their effects. It is moderate: {{fact:x.x4-paths.embstep.half_b3}} of {{fact:x.x4-paths.pairs.per_kind}} lines drawn at b3, and {{fact:x.x4-paths.embstep.half_b9}} drawn at b9, put more than half of their embedded distance into one sixteenth.

Different-class lines often contain a poorly supported point. At a line's least-supported point, only {{fact:x.x4-paths.support.b3.b9}} of clean test images are farther from their nearest probe image at b9, and {{fact:x.x4-paths.support.b3.logits}} at the logits (medians over lines drawn at b3), so the map's positions there are extrapolations.

Limits

  • One network, one kind of path. ResNet-20 seed 0, final checkpoint, straight lines between correctly classified images. It was not compared with the MLP setting where barriers were seen in earlier activation-cell work.
  • Recorded sites only. Activations are recorded at block outputs, the pooled features and the logits, not inside blocks; ReLU switches are counted at block outputs, as net changes between neighbouring points, so they are not a count of distinct cells crossed.
  • Sampling. Each line has {{fact:x.x4-paths.grid.points}} points, and logit speed and gain are computed at {{fact:x.x4-paths.grid.draw}} of them, so concentration narrower than one step could hide between points. Re-sampling {{fact:x.x4-paths.refine.n}} lines four times more finely changed no fastest-sixteenth share by more than {{fact:x.x4-paths.refine.raw_max}} percentage points.
  • The bunching in the map is checked against straight b9 lines only. Other kernels and kernel widths were not tried.

Technical details

Line. x(s) = (1 − s)a + sb, where a and b are the two images' activations at the start site as the network computes them (for pixels, the normalised input), on {{fact:x.x4-paths.grid.points}} evenly spaced points. Endpoints are {{fact:x.x4-paths.pairs.unique_endpoints}} distinct held-out test images, correctly classified with a top-two logit margin above {{fact:x.x4-paths.pairs.margin_min}}.

Fastest sixteenth. At a later site, the step lengths are the Euclidean distances between the activations of neighbouring points; the fastest sixteenth is the largest sum over a sixteenth of consecutive steps, divided by the sum of all steps. In the map it uses distances in the first {{fact:x.x4-paths.emb.k}} site-local coordinates; the b9 comparison uses the {{fact:x.x4-paths.grid.draw}}-point grid on which the map was computed for all three panels.

KL, logit speed and gain. KL(start || current) = Σc pc(0) log(pc(0)/pc(s)), with p the softmax of the logits and natural logs. Logit speed is ‖J(b − a)‖ for the Jacobian J from the start site to the logits, computed exactly by a forward-mode Jacobian–vector product; at a ReLU kink it uses the branch automatic differentiation selects. The gain is J's maximum stretch factor σmax (its largest singular value), computed exactly from J JT; the log gain is ln σmax. From b9 and from pool, log gain matches the affine closed forms to {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.affine.b9}} and {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.affine.pool}}. Exact speed and the finite difference of the line differ by a median {{fact:x.x4-paths.speedfd.pixels}} at the logits (lines drawn in pixels; {{fact:x.x4-paths.speedfd1024.pixels}} four times more finely): ReLU cells are crossed within a step.

Population numbers for the Jacobian (medians over different-class pairs). Log gain at the midpoint against the start: {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.pixels.mid}} against {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.pixels.s0}} drawn in pixels, {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.b3.mid}} against {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.b3.s0}} at b3, {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.b6.mid}} against {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.b6.s0}} at b6. Divided by the number of sites crossed, the midpoint log gain is a finite-time Lyapunov exponent of {{fact:x.x4-paths.ftle.pixels.mid}}, {{fact:x.x4-paths.ftle.b3.mid}} and {{fact:x.x4-paths.ftle.b6.mid}} per site. At the midpoint, the line's own direction receives {{fact:x.x4-paths.align.pixels.mid}}, {{fact:x.x4-paths.align.b3.mid}} and {{fact:x.x4-paths.align.b6.mid}} of the maximum stretch. Where the logits move fastest is associated with, not tied to, the first switch: drawn at b3, the rank correlation between the two positions is {{fact:x.x4-paths.timing.all}} over all different-class pairs and {{fact:x.x4-paths.timing.single}} over the {{fact:x.x4-paths.timing.single_n}} with a single switch.

Gain and confidence across the probe. At each of the {{fact:x.x4-paths.probe.n}} probe images, raw log gain from pixels to the logits has rank correlation {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.pixels.raw}} with the margin (top logit minus runner-up). A direction-only log gain, on centred and normalised activations, gives {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.pixels.angular}}, mostly because it divides by the length of the centred logit vector, which itself tracks the margin ({{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.radius}}); without that factor it gives {{fact:x.x4-paths.gain.pixels.radius_removed}}.

The direction-only distance used in the first version of this page (distances between centred, normalised activations, as the diffusion kernel sees them) gives the same answer: {{fact:x.x4-paths.pre_logits.n}} of {{fact:x.x4-paths.paths.total}} lines put more than {{fact:x.x4-paths.threshold.share}} of it into a sixteenth before the logits, {{fact:x.x4-paths.logits.n}} at the logits; refined lines changed by a per-site median of at most {{fact:x.x4-paths.refine.max_median}}. It rises near the probe mean, where normalising amplifies small moves, so this page uses raw distance. A second raw measure, comparing the largest step on a four-times coarser grid with the largest step (1 for a smooth line, 0 for a jump), has minimum {{fact:x.x4-paths.sharp.min}}.

Diffusion maps. At each site, a Shannon kernel of perplexity {{fact:x.x4-paths.emb.perplexity}} on cosines of probe-centred activations; site-local coordinates are the leading nonconstant eigenvectors of that site's own diffusion operator, and line points are placed by Nyström extension against the fixed probe. A site's coordinates do not depend on the start site, so the b9 line can be drawn in the same panels as the b3 line. The two coordinates drawn are not concentrated on a few images (at most {{fact:x.x4-paths.modes.top2_max}} of a coordinate's squared norm on its two largest images); some higher coordinates are. A class's centre (×) is the median of its probe images in the two drawn coordinates. Support is a point's largest cosine to any probe image at that site, as a percentile among clean held-out test images.

Choices that could matter. Straight lines in raw activations rather than paths along the data; the same pairs at every start; gain not divided by depth in the plots; the kernel width fixed, not tuned.
